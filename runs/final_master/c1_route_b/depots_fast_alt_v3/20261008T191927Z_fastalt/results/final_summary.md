## 2. Answers to the eight questions (plan section 15)

**Q1. How must the earlier "IPC is mostly only speed" explanation be revised?** It holds only partly. With FAST the DENSE search is 1.5-2.1x deeper in the same 300 s, and the four pre-flagged problems still fail: p06 / p11 / p12 / p17 reach 28,913 / 29,127 / 21,787 / 13,559 expansions (REF: 14,398 / 15,956 / 10,799 / 8,060), whereas the WL scorer in the same engine solves them at 593 / 1,556 / 978 / 588 expansions. FAST has passed WL's solving expansion count after 6-16 s and then searches the remaining ~285-295 s without success, so these four are a **node-level guidance disadvantage, not truncation**. p21 and p22 are still **truncated**: WL needs 25,037 / 48,652 expansions, FAST reached 1,804 / 512 (about 4,200 s / 28,500 s at the measured rates). p19 and p20 have no WL reference (WL hits the 100k node limit too), so nothing can be said about guidance there. In the other direction DENSE solves p14 and p15 where WL and h_add exhaust 100,000 nodes (`results/ipc_guidance_vs_truncation.csv`). The previous card's reading "the measured bottleneck is the cost of the learned score" therefore needs the correction above: it is right for p21/p22, wrong for p06/p11/p12/p17.

**Q2. Which cost did FAST remove, and are the verified values and prefixes preserved?** Micro-benchmark (`profile/timings.csv`, A100, median of three interleaved repeats, B=48): training-size templates 20.4-24.0 ms -> 10.7-10.9 ms (1.9-2.2x; the floor is ~10.5 ms of launch-bound relational stage); IPC p15 / p21 / p22: 140 -> 94 ms, 259 -> 186 ms, 657 -> 492 ms (1.48x / 1.40x / 1.34x). On p22 B=48 the removed time is host state packing 24 ms -> 0.2 ms, host-to-device copy 43 ms -> 0.2 ms, unused old readout 3 ms -> 0, static preparation 5.0 -> 1.7 ms and about 90 ms inside the relational section (cached per-relation edge tensors, batch plumbing); what remains is the message passing itself (487 of 492 ms), which only an exact shared / incremental computation could shrink. Numbers: packed proposition tensors, edge tensors and static embeddings are bitwise identical to the reference (6 templates, 3 batch sizes, high-bit states). Values: because the GPU scatter in message passing is not bitwise repeatable, FAST was judged against the reference's own repeat (rule registered before the main runs). Over 6,230 compared values FAST is within the plan tolerance `1e-5 + 1e-6|ref|` on all 6,230 (REF vs REF repeat: 6,228), the largest difference is 2.44e-4 for both FAST-vs-REF and REF-vs-REF repeat, 38% of values are bitwise equal for both. Prefixes: fixtures (20 Struct/Joint problems) identical to the end for 18 problems FAST vs REF (17 for REF vs REF repeat); the 2 FAST divergences occur at the same generated-batch index (17 and 22) as the divergences of the REF repeat on those problems. **On the IPC main runs the trajectories are not equal**: only 6 of 22 rolling hashes agree to the end (p01, p03, p04, p05, p07, p08); of the 14 solved, 5 diverge at a compared mark (p02, p09, p16 at 10; p14, p18 at 100) and 3 only after the last compared mark; all 8 timeouts diverge between 10 and 1,000 expansions. Per plan 7.3(7) the "trajectory-equivalent" interpretation is therefore **revoked for those 16 rows**; the IPC main matrix contains no REF repeat, so how much of that divergence is the reference's own nondeterminism is not measured directly there (indirect evidence: IPC natural batches have the same full-order agreement for FAST-vs-REF and REF-vs-REF repeat, 14% vs 22% on p22 and 3% vs 3% on p21; REF-vs-old-DENSE of the previous card also differs on 2/14 plan lengths and 1/14 expansion counts, FAST-vs-REF on 3/14 and 0/14).

**Q3. What did FAST explore in the same time, and what happened to completion and plan quality?** In the 8 problems where both time out at 300 s, FAST reaches 1.46-2.12x (median 1.75x) the expansions; per-problem scoring throughput (states per scoring-second) is 1.37-2.25x (median 1.83x, aggregate 1.74x: 1,098,185 vs 643,675 states). Both orders (REF first / FAST first) give the same ratio (1.79 / 1.89), so no order artefact is visible. On the 14 problems both solve, wall time is 95.6 s (REF) vs 56.1 s (FAST); the three problems with REF wall >= 5 s speed up 1.76x (median). Completion is **unchanged**: the same 14 problems, 11/14/14 at 10/60/300 s, no problem gained or lost; every common problem needs the same number of expansions (14 of 14); plan lengths are equal on 11, FAST shorter on 1 and longer on 2 (sign test p = 1), mean plan length 37.43 vs 37.36. Costs: FAST preparation 14.0 s vs 7.5 s over 22 problems (+0.3 s per problem, inside the problem clock), cache <= 251 MiB (cap 256 MiB), GPU peak 2.0 vs 2.3 GiB, RSS growth 0.22 vs 0.18 GiB. The gain is largest on small graphs and smallest on the largest ones (p22: 1.34x per call at B=48), i.e. just where the unsolved problems are.

**Q4. What does ALT_DENSE repair and damage against single DENSE; is the good Joint plan quality kept?** IPC against FAST_DENSE (identical result against REF_DENSE): **repairs p11 and p21, loses p15** (15 vs 14). p11: 8,452 expansions in 94.5 s (single DENSE: 29,127 expansions, TIMEOUT); p21: 46 expansions in 17.6 s (single DENSE: 1,804 expansions, TIMEOUT); p15: single DENSE solves it at 804 expansions in 15-27 s, ALT reaches 18,551 expansions in 300 s without a solution. On the 13 commonly solved problems plans are shorter 7 / longer 2 / equal 4 (p = 0.18); expansions are fewer on 6 and more on 7. Joint: completion kept (128/128), but plans are longer on 64 and shorter on 15 problems than OLD_DENSE, optimal plans 72 -> 40 and the mean ratio 1.0945 -> 1.1523 (above the protection line, see section 1); Struct: 25 -> 16 optimal plans, 1.0218 -> 1.0637. So the good plan quality is **partly** kept: ALT_DENSE is still clearly better than WL and h_add on Struct/Joint (ratio and expansions), but it gives up a good part of what single DENSE has.

**Q5. Against ALT_WL and single h_add, does DENSE add value or only compute?** *Against single h_add (EAGER_HADD / OLD_HADD)*: real value in quality and node counts: IPC 15 vs 10 solved (+p08, p09, p14, p16, p18, none lost), Joint ratio 1.1523 vs 1.4849, Struct 1.0637 vs 1.2957; but 10.5x fewer expansions (8,859 vs 92,936 on 10 common problems) come with 4.2x more wall time (117.6 s vs 28.1 s). *Against the same schedule with WL (ALT_WL_ADD)*: on Struct/Joint DENSE adds quality (Joint 78 shorter / 32 longer, p = 1.4e-5, and 123 of 128 problems with fewer expansions), but in transport-needing Joint problems the advantage disappears (ALT_DENSE 1.2092 vs ALT_WL 1.2127, both worse than single WL 1.1648); on IPC ALT_WL solves 18 vs 15 (p06, p12, p17, p22 only ALT_WL; p14 only ALT_DENSE). The two problems ALT repairs for DENSE (p11, p21) are both solved much faster by h_add alone (10 s and 3 s), so there is **no evidence that the DENSE+h_add combination is more than h_add's own success paid for with neural cost**; p14 (83 expansions vs 1,289 for DENSE alone while h_add and WL fail) is a single-problem hint of complementarity, not a result. Adding h_add to WL changed nothing in coverage (ALT_WL = EAGER_WL = 18, same set) and lengthened plans (13 longer / 3 shorter on 18 common problems, Joint ratio 1.2498 vs 1.2186).

**Q6. Conclusions by expansions, state evaluations, expensive forwards and time: any conflict?** Yes, one per comparison. FAST vs REF: all four measures agree (same expansions; 1.74x states per scoring-second; 1.5-2.1x expansions per 300 s; 1.7x wall on solved problems). ALT_DENSE vs FAST on the 13 common IPC problems: expansions 899 vs 2,095, DENSE-scored states 11,097 vs 21,337, encoder forwards 899 vs 2,095, wall 25.5 s vs 40.9 s - all favour ALT, **but the whole difference is p14** (83 vs 1,289 expansions); per problem ALT has fewer expansions on 6 and more on 7. ALT_DENSE vs EAGER_HADD: 10.5x fewer expansions, 6x fewer scored states, but 4.2x more wall time. ALT_DENSE vs EAGER_WL (14 common): 4.3x fewer expansions, 14x fewer scored states (56,292 vs 787,038) but 1.8x more wall time (135 s vs 75 s; GPU vs CPU resources). Struct/Joint against OLD_DENSE (counts only, old time is another day): ALT_DENSE needs 1.37x more expansions (4,276 vs 3,124 on Joint; 628 vs 458 on Struct) and 1.29x more DENSE-scored states. h_add adds ~7% to the scoring time in ALT_DENSE on IPC (147 s of 2,195 s). So "fewer expansions" or "fewer forwards" is not a time result and, against single DENSE, not even a count result on Struct/Joint.

**Q7. Which claims are supported, which are hypotheses, which stop?** See `claim_boundary.md`. Supported (development evidence, one seed): FAST is an equivalent-value speed-up with unchanged IPC coverage; DENSE keeps its same-family plan-quality and node advantage over WL and h_add on Struct/Joint; DENSE has a node-level guidance disadvantage to WL on p06/p11/p12/p17. Hypotheses: that the cause is training-world topology; that ALT has problem-specific complementarity (p14); that p21/p22 are guidance problems. Stop: the 1:1 alternating recipe (ALT_DENSE and ALT_WL), the claim that FAST improves coverage, and "IPC is only speed".

**Q8. Single priority for the next card?** **A topology-coverage training card** (design in `method_recommendation.md`; not started, needs your authorization). Not exact cost reduction: the four guidance-failure problems already ran 13k-29k expansions, and p21/p22 need 14x / 95x more expansions than FAST reached, which a further 1.3-2x does not close. Not an independent-confirmation-first card: what is open (guidance on unfamiliar worlds) can only be tested on new independently generated problems, which the topology card must produce anyway.

## 3. What differs from the registered plan, and amendments after the registration commit
- **Value rule.** The plan's tolerance is reported as asked (FAST 6,230/6,230 within); the pass/fail rule used for `fast_status` was the noise-calibrated one registered in `plan/registration.json` (max|FAST-REF| <= 2 max|REF-REF repeat| + 1e-4 per template and group; prefix divergences of FAST <= those of the REF repeat + 2). Result: `PASS`, so FAST was used for ALT_DENSE and for the IPC timing blocks.
- **Prefix runs**: 60 instead of the literal 40 (REF repeat added, see above).
- **Engine**: `search_match/engine.py` gained a read-only `observer=None` hook (3 lines) used for the rolling expansion hashes; with the default the search is unchanged (hash check against the registered file is `SAME`, the fixtures of the previous card pass).
- **After the registration commit** only `report.py` (additional generated tables: `fast_vs_ref_by_case.csv`, `ipc_guidance_vs_truncation.csv`, `alt_cost_accounting.csv`, `strata_struct_joint.csv`) and the receipt details in `scripts/c1_depots_fast_alt.py` (`cmd_final_receipt`) were changed; the scoring, engine, budget, runner, config and test files are byte-identical to the registered hashes.
- IPC GPU trio (REF / FAST / ALT_DENSE) were timed back to back on one GPU per problem (order by case_id hash, ALT_DENSE last); WL / h_add / ALT_WL ran on CPU (4 threads) in other tasks, so IPC wall times between GPU and CPU rows are indicative only. No condition was rerun; shared-machine load is recorded per row (`load_avg_1m`).
- Cold start (outside every problem clock, as in the previous card): weight loading 0.4-0.9 s per worker plus a CUDA / kernel warm-up on a Train96 problem; the per-problem FAST preparation (static embedding, cached edge tensors) is inside the clock.
- p-values (`paired_quality.csv`) are uncorrected sign tests on development problems with one seed; large p does not prove equality, small p does not prove a general mechanism.
